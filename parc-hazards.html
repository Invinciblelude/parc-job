Hazard reviews

Process. Prevention. Proof. I follow PARC’s procedure for this task. I check the control before someone gets hurt. I document it so it proves what we actually did. If a required control is missing: pause work, notify supervisor and Director.

1. Asbestos / lead containment looks sloppy

Inhalation and take-home contamination. I check the work plan, PPE, respirator status, cards, containment, decon, and housekeeping. If a required control is missing, I pause that task. I do not redesign the abatement method. Technical calls go to the Director or the competent person.

2. Crew entering without a current fit test / wrong respirator

That is pause work, not a later email. Medical evaluation, fit, training, and the right selection under PARC’s program have to be current. A card is worthless with the wrong facepiece, a bad seal, or facial hair defeating the seal. I do not recertify them on the bumper.

3. Unknown drums / tanker / dump / mercury / PCB

I do not assume the product or the PPE. Size-up: what it might be, wind, drainage, people at risk, still live vs already stabilized. Boundaries. Notify response leadership. Confirm trained people for the function. Awareness notifies. Operations is defensive. Technician is offensive. I work the level PARC assigns. Drum in a truck is not closeout.

4. Confined space “we always do it this way”

Low oxygen, toxics, engulfment, failed rescue. I confirm permit, testing, ventilation, attendant, communication, rescue, authorized entrants. Missing any of that is pause. I do not interpret the atmosphere myself if that is not my assigned job.

5. Broken ladder / harness / extinguisher still on the truck

Tag it out, notify the supervisor, request replacement, write the time. The inspection form is not compliance if the gear is still in service.

6. Heat on an abatement or debris job

Heat illness is an operational hazard: water, rest, shade, acclimatization, supervisor watch, emergency plan. Dizzy, nauseated, or unable to continue is pause. I do not treat it as toughness.

7. Injury or near miss

Care first. Control the immediate hazard. Notify supervisor, Director, and HR through PARC’s process. Facts only: time, task, PPE, witnesses, photos, what I did. Owner, due date, verify closed. I do not diagnose, blame, or decide reportability alone. A fatality or potentially serious injury can start the employer’s Cal/OSHA clock. I escalate immediately. I do not wait until Monday.

8. Superintendent: “We’re behind — just go / just sign it”

I hear the schedule. A required control is missing. Here is what we need to resume: replacement gear, the right trained person, or a Director decision. I paused this task, not the whole site. I will not sign or upload what I cannot verify. If they keep pushing, the Director already has the facts.

9. “Just throw it on the truck” / bad label / no manifest

Mislabel and incompatible waste are spills waiting to happen. Assigned people, packaging, labels, documents, equipment. Waste profile questions go up. I do not invent a code to keep the truck moving.

10. ISN / Avetta: “Just upload something”

That is the customer’s gate, not OSHA. Current, true records get the crew on site. A fake upload is worse than a delay. I have not run the portal yet. Show me the account and who may upload. I flag gaps before mobilization. I never submit what I cannot verify.

11. “You’re the IH — just run the sample / pick the cartridge”

I will not pretend I already run industrial hygiene. I will not guess a method, a cartridge, or a result. After PARC trains and authorizes me, I own calibration, the sample, chain of custody, the log, and telling the affected employee. Until then I coordinate and I ask.

12. Demo / coatings: silica, falls, moving equipment

Walk access, fall protection, ladders, cords, moving equipment, dust, hearing protection, task plan. Same loop. Tag defects. Name an owner.

13. Worker reports a hazard, threat, or “do it anyway” order

They can report without retaliation. I take it, record it, act, notify. Violence: get people safe, follow PARC’s workplace-violence process, escalate. I do not make a private deal to skip PPE.

14. “Are we compliant? If someone gets hurt, that’s on you.”

PARC owns the safety program. My job is to identify hazards, use the written process, document facts, pause or correct what is in my authority, and escalate fast. I do not certify the company. I do not freelance legal conclusions. I never falsify or conceal.

Closer if they stack two hazards: I will identify it, control or pause it, notify the Director, and close it on paper only after it is closed in the field. Teach me PARC’s pumps, fit-test machine, and ISN. I will not fake those.
