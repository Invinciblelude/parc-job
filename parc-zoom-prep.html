E1 · “You’re the safety guy — if someone gets hurt, that’s on you.”

PARC, as the employer, owns the safety program and company compliance obligations. My duty is to perform my assigned work competently: identify hazards, use the written process, document facts, correct or pause what is within my authority, and escalate promptly. I do not make legal conclusions. I never falsify, backdate, conceal, or represent a condition as compliant without verification.

E2 · “What do employees get from you?”

Five things: a safe start; a voice without retaliation; fast action — tag out, pause, escalate; a fair response focused on cause not blame; closed-loop follow-up with an owner, due date, and proof. If they report a danger to me, I take it seriously, record it, act, and notify the right people.

E3 · Crew entering hot zone without respirator / expired fit

That is pause work, not a later email. A required control is missing. Notify supervisor and Director. Resume only when program conditions are met: medical evaluation, fit testing, training, and proper selection under PARC’s respiratory program. A signed fit-test card is worthless if they are wearing the wrong facepiece, have a bad seal, or facial hair defeating the seal. I do not recertify them on the bumper and I do not let it go “this once.” I track authorized clearance and fit-test status — I do not handle medical diagnoses.

E4 · Superintendent: “We’re behind — just sign it / just go.”

I would stay respectful and factual. I would name the missing control, not criticize the person. If the work cannot be performed safely, I pause it and give the team a practical path to resume — replacement equipment, the right trained person, a corrected work plan, or a Director decision. I will not sign or upload something I cannot verify, and I will create a clear written record of the issue and escalation.

E5 · Unknown drums / tanker / dump / mercury / PCB

I do not assume identity or PPE. Size-up: product if known, wind, drainage, people, still uncontrolled vs post-emergency cleanup. Boundaries. Notify response leadership. Confirm the plan and that people are trained for the function — awareness vs operations vs technician vs command are not the same card. My FRO/decon background is defensive field foundation. I will not claim I stop the leak at the source unless PARC assigned me technician. After: photos, injury/near miss, chain of custody, manifests. Drum full is not closed.

E6 · “Have you done air sampling / are you an IH?”

I will not pretend I already run IH. I will not guess a method, a cartridge, or a waste profile. Pause and ask the Director or the assigned competent person. After PARC trains, authorizes, and supervises me, I own calibration, the sample, chain of custody, the log, and telling the affected employee. Until then I coordinate and learn. Guessing is how people get exposed and how I create my own professional risk.

E7 · Confined space they “always do this way”

Low oxygen, toxics, engulfment, failed rescue. I confirm permit, atmospheric testing, ventilation, attendant, communication, rescue, authorized entrants. Missing any of that is pause work, not a toolbox talk after lunch. Technical interpretation goes up.

E8 · Broken ladder still on the truck / inspection already signed

A completed form is not compliance if the ladder is still in service. I tag it out, notify the supervisor, request replacement, write the time. Same for a damaged harness, cord, or extinguisher. Proof means the control is actually in place.

E9 · Injury or near miss on a spill

First, I make sure the employee gets care and the immediate hazard is controlled. I pause related work when needed, notify the supervisor and EHS Director immediately, and follow PARC’s HR and injury-reporting process. I capture factual details — time, task, PPE, witnesses, photos, conditions, and what was done to control the hazard. Then we assign a corrective action, owner, due date, and verify closure. I do not diagnose, blame the employee, or decide reportability alone.

E10 · Heat on an abatement or debris job

Heat illness is an operational hazard: water, rest, shade, acclimatization, supervisor monitoring, emergency plan. Symptomatic or unable to continue is pause. I do not treat heat as “they’ll tough it out.”

E11 · Waste load / manifest / “just throw it on”

Mislabel, incompatible waste, spill, bad manifest. I verify assigned people, packaging, labels, documents, equipment. Waste classification and transport questions go to the authorized person. I do not invent a profile to keep the truck moving.

E12 · ISN/Avetta “just upload something”

ISN is a contractor prequalification and compliance platform that customers use to verify whether contractors meet their safety, insurance, training, and documentation requirements before crews work on site. It is not OSHA. I have not administered it yet. I would learn PARC’s account ownership, customer-specific requirements, document-approval process, and expiration calendar. My job would be to help make sure Sacramento records are current and accurate before mobilization. An upload is not automatically an approval. I would never submit a document I cannot verify. ISN is the customer’s contractor-compliance gate: current records help the crew get on site; inaccurate records create safety, business, and liability problems.

E13 · Worker reports a threat or unsafe order

Voice without retaliation is IIPP. Violence: follow the workplace-violence plan, emergency help if needed, document, escalate. Pressure to skip PPE is stop/escalate, not a private deal with the supervisor.

E14 · “How do you walk a job?”

People — who is performing this task, and are they authorized, trained, equipped, and fit for it? Hazard — what can harm someone right now? Control — what physically prevents harm? Proof — is the record accurate and current? Escalation — is this outside my authority or technical competence? Closure — has the corrective action actually been completed and verified? A signed form with a broken ladder still on the truck is not a walk.

E15 · Asbestos/lead containment looks wrong

Inhalation, dirty clothes and tools, failed decon. I check work plan, PPE, respiratory program status, cards, containment/decon, housekeeping, records. I do not redesign the abatement method. Technical questions go to the Director or competent person. I can still pause if a control is missing.

E16 · “Are we compliant?”

I do not certify the company. I will make the Sacramento operation easier to audit and safer to run. A checklist does not make a job safe. I want field controls to be real.

L5b · If they press RAVS, EMR, Avetta, or a flagged account

RAVS is ISN’s written-program review. Reviewers check the document against the hiring client’s checklist. Uploaded is not accepted. Deficiency notes go to the Director — I do not rewrite the company program on my own. Avetta is the same kind of private customer gate, often with more client-specific questionnaires; clearing one does not clear the other. EMR comes from workers’ compensation claims, not from the portal; I do not try to improve it by hiding injuries. Accounts get flagged for expired insurance, stale 300A, failed program review, expired training, or assuming upload meant approval. I flag gaps before mobilization.

P1 · Cal/OSHA serious-injury clock

Care comes first. Pause related work and notify the supervisor and EHS Director immediately; notify HR through PARC’s injury-reporting process. A fatality or potentially serious work-related injury or illness may trigger an immediate Cal/OSHA reporting obligation, with an outside limit of eight hours for the employer. I preserve the facts and escalate right away — I do not wait, diagnose, or make the reportability call alone. Serious includes inpatient hospitalization other than observation or diagnostic testing, amputation, loss of an eye, or serious permanent disfigurement. The company makes that determination. I do not personally report to Cal/OSHA unless assigned. A near miss is not that clock unless it also meets those criteria. PARC owns recordkeeping. If assigned, I gather complete facts and support accurate, timely entry; I do not independently decide recordability beyond my authority.

P2 · Role limits

I assist the Director. I will identify, document, correct, pause, or escalate based on PARC’s process and my authority. I will learn PARC’s technical procedures before acting beyond my training. I do not redesign the program, certify compliance, approve every restart, guess IH or waste decisions, manage medical files, or take incident command unless assigned.

P3 · Near-miss file

Treat potential severity, not luck. Same loop: control the hazard, notify, facts, owner, due date, verify closure. Write what happened and what was done — not “they were reckless.” Share the learning without name-and-shame. If it repeats, escalate as a system issue. Separate care from blame and from claim administration.

P4 · Director note after a pause

Time, location, missing control, who is affected, what I paused, what we need to resume, photos if I have them. No opinions. Example: 09:20 Vinci south scaffold — Type I ladder left rail loose — removed from service — supervisor notified — replacement requested — photos attached.
