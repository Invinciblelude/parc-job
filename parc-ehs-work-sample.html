Spills and disasters — how and why I am effective

Study this

I am effective on spills and disasters because I already run the same loop under stress: size-up, named accountability, PPE and zone discipline, decon, crew briefing, and closeout. That is operations-level field habit (FRO, decon, WMD, 17 years of emergency-response leadership). The EHS Specialist job is to wrap PARC’s safety system around that work so crews can respond legally and the file is complete — not to replace fire command, the project manager, a HazMat technician, or the Director.

Emergency vs post-emergency (say this correctly): Cal/OSHA HAZWOPER (8 CCR 5192) treats an emergency as an uncontrolled — or potentially uncontrolled — hazardous-substance release that can create high toxicity or immediate danger. Once the release is stabilized or eliminated, remaining work is post-emergency response: planned cleanup under the employer’s safety program. PARC lives in both. I do not treat “the fire department left” as “safety is over.”

HAZWOPER functions (not the same card): Awareness — recognize, notify, do not take action beyond that. Operations (FRO) — defensive, from a safe distance, protect people/property/environment, do not stop the leak at the source unless trained and assigned as technician. Technician — offensive, stop or contain at the source. Incident command — structure, safety, resources. I will work PARC’s assigned level. I will not claim technician or IC because I hold FRO/decon/leadership.

PPE levels (only if asked — PARC’s program decides, not me): D — work clothes, no respirator, nuisance only. C — air-purifying respirator when the atmosphere is known and filters work. B — SCBA plus chemical suit, high respiratory, less skin than A. A — fully encapsulating vapor-tight, SCBA, highest skin and vapor. I do not pick Level A on my own in the interview. I say: known vs unknown atmosphere, PARC’s IAP and competent person, Director oversight.

ICS (high level): Incident Commander, Operations, Planning, Logistics, Finance/Admin, and a Safety Officer on larger responses. The EHS Specialist is not automatically Safety Officer for every PARC call. I support the safety system the company names.
